§ 01Who we are

Engineers from the island, for the island

The Nuclear Alternative Project (NAP) is a 501(c)(3) nonprofit created by a group of Puerto Rican engineers working across the U.S. nuclear industry. We came together around a shared belief: Puerto Rico deserves an energy future that is clean, affordable, and strong enough to withstand the next storm.

Our mission is to educate communities in Puerto Rico about nuclear energy in a way that facilitates the free and open exchange of information — so that decisions about the island's energy future are made with facts, not fear.

"A strong and modern Puerto Rico, powered by a clean energy portfolio that includes nuclear."

§ 02The Challenge

A grid pushed to its limits

When Hurricane María struck in 2017, it exposed how fragile Puerto Rico's aging, fossil-fuel-dependent grid truly is. Rebuilding stronger means rethinking how the island generates power.

01

Imported fossil fuels

The island relies heavily on imported oil and gas, exposing families and industry to volatile, high electricity prices.

02

Vulnerable to storms

Centralized plants and long transmission lines are easily knocked out — leaving communities dark for weeks after a hurricane.

03

Clean-energy goals

Puerto Rico has committed to 100% renewable electricity — a goal that needs firm, carbon-free power to stay reliable day and night.

04

Resilience by design

The path forward is a web of microgrids that can keep hospitals, water, and homes running when the grid goes down.

§ 03Our Work

From an idea to a national research effort

What began as volunteer engineers sharing a vision is now a multi-year program funded by the U.S. Department of Energy and supported by national laboratories.

  1. 2015

    The team comes together

    Puerto Rican engineers working in the U.S. nuclear industry unite around a shared mission to study advanced reactors for the island.

  2. 2019

    DOE feasibility grant

    The U.S. Department of Energy Office of Nuclear Energy funds NAP to study market conditions, the legal framework, existing infrastructure, and public perception.

  3. 2020

    Landmark feasibility study

    NAP publishes the "Preliminary Feasibility Study for SMRs and Microreactors for Puerto Rico," concluding that small reactors could replace up to 74% of the island's existing power plants — economically and technically feasible.

  4. 2021+

    Phase 2: siting study

    With over $1.6M in additional DOE funding, NAP advances to a site-suitability study, identifying two candidate sites using Nuclear Regulatory Commission criteria.

§ 05The Technology

Advanced reactors, right-sized for an island

Modern nuclear isn't the giant plants of the past. Advanced reactors are smaller, factory-built, and designed to be inherently safe — a natural fit for Puerto Rico's grid and geography.

Small Modular Reactor≤ 300 MW

SMR

Reactors up to ~300 MW per unit, built in factories and shipped to site. Scalable to match demand and ideal for replacing retiring fossil plants.

  • Factory-built & scalable
  • Firm, carbon-free power 24/7
  • Pairs with solar & wind
Microreactor≤ 20 MW

Micro

Very small reactors (often under 20 MW) that can power a hospital, a town, or a microgrid — and keep running when the main grid fails.

  • Perfect for microgrids
  • Transportable & fast to deploy
  • Years of fuel without refueling

Resilience

Distributed advanced reactors complement a solar-dominated grid, stabilize prices for the manufacturing economy, and stay standing through hurricane season.

  • Storm-hardened design
  • Stable electricity prices
  • Energy independence

§ 08Straight Answers

Nuclear, honestly

The questions people ask us most — answered plainly.

Is nuclear energy safe?

Nuclear is one of the safest ways to make electricity. Advanced reactors add passive safety features that shut the reactor down naturally — without operators or external power — making a serious accident extremely unlikely.

What about nuclear waste?

Modern reactors produce small volumes of waste that are safely contained and monitored. Many advanced designs use fuel far more efficiently, and the total waste from decades of operation is remarkably compact.

Can reactors survive a hurricane?

Yes. Reactors are among the most storm-hardened structures ever built, engineered to withstand extreme winds, flooding, and seismic events — and to keep delivering power when the rest of the grid is down.

Does this replace solar and wind?

No — it complements them. Advanced nuclear provides firm, round-the-clock power that pairs with Puerto Rico's growing solar to create a clean grid that stays reliable even at night or during storms.

Is this actually happening?

NAP's work is research and education. Any deployment would require extensive study, community input, and regulatory approval. Our role is to provide the facts so Puerto Rico can decide for itself.
